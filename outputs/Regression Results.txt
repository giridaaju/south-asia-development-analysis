Regression Results
The regression analysis examines the relationship between annual GDP per capita growth and three human development indicators across eight South Asian countries between 2000 and 2024. Three model specifications were estimated: pooled ordinary least squares, country fixed effects, and country and year fixed effects.
The results provide limited evidence of a statistically significant contemporaneous association between economic growth and the selected human development indicators. In the country and year fixed-effects specification, the coefficient on GDP per capita growth was 0.005 for life expectancy, -0.241 for electricity access, and 0.294 for secondary school enrollment. None of these estimates was statistically significant at the 5% level.
The results suggest that annual economic growth, considered independently of country-specific characteristics and common year effects, does not consistently correspond with short-term changes in the selected human development indicators in this sample. Country-specific differences and time-related changes account for considerably more variation in the outcomes than GDP growth alone.
These findings should be interpreted cautiously. The analysis uses only eight country clusters, has incomplete observations for secondary school enrollment, and does not address potential reverse causality or other sources of endogeneity. Furthermore, annual GDP growth may not adequately capture the longer-term relationship between economic development and improvements in human development.
Further analysis using lagged economic growth measures and additional explanatory variables could provide a more nuanced understanding of these relationships.
